Typical problems we fix

Small inventory gaps become big margin surprises.

Most of the money in a distribution business sits on the shelf. The controller's job is to make sure what the books say is there is actually there, and that what it cost includes everything it took to get it there.

The system says we have it; the shelf says we don't.

Shrink is found once a year, at the count.

Questions

Straight answers.

Should we be on perpetual or periodic inventory?

Most distributors with a stock system benefit from perpetual inventory, because COGS and margins are right every month. Periodic can work for very simple operations. We look at your volume and systems and recommend one.

How often should we cycle count?

A common approach counts the highest-value and fastest-moving items most often and slower items less often, so the full range is covered across the year without stopping the warehouse.

We hold customer-owned or consigned stock. How is it handled?

It is tracked separately and kept off your balance sheet when it isn't yours, and stock you place with customers stays on your books until it is sold. We set up the tracking so both are clear.

Do you work inside our warehouse or inventory system?

We work from its reports and exports and reconcile them to your books. Where the data is missing something we need, we tell you exactly what and why.
