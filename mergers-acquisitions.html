Deal accounting under ASC 805

How the purchase price gets allocated.

Under US GAAP (ASC 805, with IFRS 3 as the close equivalent), a business combination is recorded in steps: identify the acquirer, set the acquisition date, measure what was paid (cash, shares and any earnout at fair value), then measure the identifiable assets acquired and liabilities assumed at fair value. Intangibles such as customer relationships, trade names and technology are recognized separately from goodwill, and inventory is stepped up to fair value, which raises cost of sales as it is sold after closing.

Goodwill is what is left: total consideration minus the fair value of net identifiable assets. If that number is negative, it is a bargain purchase; the values are re-checked and any remaining gain is recognized in earnings. Provisional amounts can be adjusted for up to one year (the measurement period) as facts that existed at the deal date become known.

Questions

Straight answers.

Can you value my company?

We prepare the financial analysis and models that support a valuation: normalized earnings, multiples, discounted cash flow and asset-based views. We do not issue formal or certified appraisals or fairness opinions. If your deal, lender or a legal matter requires one, we prepare the package for the valuation specialist.

When should a seller start preparing?

Ideally a year or more before going to market, so buyers see clean monthly history and a clear add-back schedule. Even a few months of clean-up makes diligence faster.

How is an earnout accounted for?

For the buyer, the earnout is measured at fair value on the deal date as part of the price. If it is a liability, it is remeasured each period and changes go through earnings. That is why the contract's definitions of revenue or EBITDA need to match how the books are kept.

Do you advise on asset versus stock purchase?

Deal tax structure is coordinated with your tax and legal advisors. We prepare the numbers they need to compare the options.
