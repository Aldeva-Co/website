Lender reporting

Commissions, covenants and your lender.

SaaS companies and insurance agencies look different, but lenders read their numbers the same way. Here is how the commission accounting flows through to the covenant tests and the borrowing base.

  • Commissions paid up frontAn insurance agency pays agents with a 12-month chargeback window; a SaaS company pays reps on multi-year contracts. Both are recorded as prepaid commission and expensed over the window or the contract.
  • Profit covenantsLenders test average profit over a few months. Expense the commission all at once and a healthy month can fail the test; spread it correctly and the result reflects the business.
  • Borrowing baseRecurring revenue, or commissions due from carriers, less anything past 60 days, times the lender's multiple. Every late invoice shrinks what you can borrow.
  • Lender package, right awayBooks current every day, covenants tracked three months ahead and late invoices flagged early, so the monthly package is ready when the month ends.

Questions

Straight answers.

Do we need revenue software for ASC 606?

Not always. At many growing SaaS companies, revenue schedules built from your billing data and kept in a controlled workbook are enough. When volume or contract complexity calls for a revenue tool, we help you choose and set it up.

Our billing MRR doesn't match the P&L. Is that normal?

It's common. Billing systems count what was invoiced; the books should show what was earned. We reconcile the two and give you a bridge so both numbers can be explained.

Can you get us ready for an audit or a funding round?

Yes. We build the revenue support, reconciliations and schedules auditors and investors ask for, and keep them current every month so diligence is a request, not a project.

Do you work with our existing accountant or fractional CFO?

Yes. We take the revenue accounting and close workload; your people keep the judgment calls and the relationships.
